How it works. Pinnacle is the sharpest book, so its price with the vig removed is the best estimate of each side's real chance (Pinnacle fair). EV = that chance times what you'd be paid, minus your stake: +3% means you expect to make 3¢ per $1 over the long run. For Kalshi / Robinhood, the price is the ask plus Kalshi's taker fee (about 1.75¢ at 50¢). Robinhood charges a flat 2¢ per contract instead, so knock about a point off there. Sportsbook prices are their posted moneylines. NBA player props (points, rebounds, assists, threes) come from Novig, ProphetX, Polymarket and the sportsbooks, compared at the exact same line against Pinnacle's no-vig price (power method), or, when Pinnacle doesn't list the player, the average of BetOnline / FanDuel / DraftKings (less reliable: shown under the bet). They're pulled once a game, about 2 to 3 hours before tip. Exchange fees other than Kalshi's are not included. Stake = a quarter of the Kelly bet for your bankroll, the standard way to size +EV without big swings. Best price per bet groups the same bet across your books and shows the best one, with the others beside it; every book lists each price. Odds range is in American odds (−400 to +600 by default). Every filter is saved on this device. Read it with care. Prices refresh with each run (hourly, or press ↻ Rerun), Pinnacle at most every 50 minutes, so a big edge can be a stale line that's already moved. Check the live price before you bet. "Alt line" = Kalshi's strike differs from Pinnacle's, so the fair is adjusted and less exact. Kalshi asks are only good for the size posted at that price.

CLV (closing-line value) = what the bet was worth at Pinnacle's last price before the game. Beating the close over and over is the proof an edge is real: it shows up after a couple hundred bets, long before wins and losses settle it. Units = 1u flat on every flagged side at the price it was flagged.
